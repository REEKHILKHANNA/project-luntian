A proposal for Globe Telecom

An AI that speaks Filipino, owned by Globe, running on green power

Luntian means green in Tagalog. The plan is simple: Globe adapts a free, open AI model to Filipino, Taglish and Cebuano, runs it in the Philippines on renewable power, and starts small: more is added only when it pays.

Chapter 2: the idea

Adapt a model instead of building one

It is like hiring a bright graduate and training them for the job, instead of raising someone from birth. Globe owns the result.

Chapter 3: how it works

Each question goes to the right-sized model

At the start, one medium-sized Globe model answers almost everything, which costs less and uses less energy than sending every question to a giant model. A small fast model and a large one come later, only if they pay.

An outside AI is used only as a rare backup for very hard cases, and it never sees personal data.

Chapter 5: training

A school for Luntian

Every new version goes through the same steps, so quality, privacy and energy are checked the same way each time. The first version is made during Steps 1 to 3. After that, Luntian gets one full refresh a year, or two smaller ones.

Chapter 6: where it lives

A locked room, on renewable power

Globe does not build a data centre. It starts with one server in a small locked space of about 15 kW at STT GDC Philippines, the data centre company Globe co-owns, and adds servers only when they are busy.

Chapter 7: the people

A team of 12

Ten start in Step 1 and two more join in Step 2. After going live, a team of 8 runs Luntian. Up to half can come from AIDE, Globe's AI group, and Globe IT. Partners such as AI Singapore, Singapore's national AI programme, and Philippine universities work alongside at first, then hand the skills over to Globe.

Chapter 8: safety

Customer data stays in the Philippines

Globe owns what is built, and every new version is checked before anyone uses it.

  • Personal data never leaves the Philippines.Names, numbers and addresses are hidden before training, and SIM registration records are never used.
  • Globe owns it.The models, the Filipino data, the test questions and the platform all belong to Globe, and partner contracts say so.
  • It is locked away.Only named staff can enter, and there is no public internet path to the models.
  • It is tested before release.Every new version is checked for quality, safety and bias, then watched every day.
  • It has its own safety guard.A separate model checks questions and answers in Filipino and Taglish, and blocks harmful ones.
  • Outsiders check it.An ethics board with outside members, and an outside red-team audit and penetration test before customers see it. ISO/IEC 42001, the international standard for managing AI, is added when business clients or regulators ask for it.

Chapter 9: the plan

Three steps to go live in 12 months

Before Step 1, a 4-week planning study gets the first step ready. After Steps 1 and 2, Globe's leaders meet at a gate, look at real results and decide whether to release the next amount of money. If a test fails, Globe stops early and keeps what was built.

Chapter 11: proof it works

Other telecom companies have done this

The closest example is next door, in Indonesia, and two of them started small.

Indonesia

Indosat and GoTo

Adapted open models into Sahabat-AI, an Indonesian-language model. One version was trained in 7 days on 32 GPUs.

Indosat now sells AI services to more than 20 companies

Switzerland

Swisscom

Offers Apertus, a Swiss-built open model, to its business customers.

Available to business customers from launch day in September 2025

United States

AT&T

Moved routine work from a big model to small, specially trained ones.

The same work at 35% of the previous cost, with 91% accuracy

Italy

Fastweb

Started by tuning an open model on 8 GPUs, then built an Italian model with licensed data, an ethics board and an outside audit.

Sold to businesses as FastwebAI Suite

The lesson: start from an open model, start small, keep it in your own country, and sell it to businesses once it works.

Chapter 12: growth

It grows only where there is real demand

Luntian starts in customer care chat. Each new branch starts only when its trigger is met and its own numbers show that it pays.

More computing power

When the first server is busy more than 70% of the time for four weeks

Add a second 8-GPU server, about US$0.44M a year

More channels

When care chat meets its targets for three months in a row

Add voice on the care hotline, about US$0.80M once

More languages

When there is enough licensed data and the tests are good

Add Ilocano, Hiligaynon and Waray, about US$0.37M once

Business clients

When at least three banks, hospitals or agencies sign letters of intent

A private Luntian for each, about US$0.40M once, plus about US$0.28M for a standby site and ISO/IEC 42001. Each client's servers are paid from its fees

Smaller and bigger models

When the anti-scam team asks, or more than 5% of questions need the outside backup

A small model for scam warnings, about US$0.26M once, and a large one for the hardest questions, about US$0.32M once plus a server

Chapter 13: how to start

Two decisions, then the first checkpoint

Nothing more is committed until each gate is passed.

From Globe, Step 1 needs four things: a senior sponsor; up to five people from Globe's AI group and IT, if they can be spared; permission to check the care data for privacy, with about 1,000 real care questions; and early talks with STT GDC Philippines about the small locked space needed from Step 2.
